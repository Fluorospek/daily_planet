---
title: Council approves arena budget in 7-2 vote
date: 2026-08-03
author: Lois Lane
section: City
publication: Daily Planet
---
The city council voted 7 to 2 on Monday night to approve a 480 million dollar budget for the new downtown arena, ending months of debate over one of the largest public projects in the city's history.

The approved plan commits 320 million dollars in public funds, with the remaining 160 million to be raised through private investment and naming-rights deals. Construction is expected to begin in the spring of 2027, with the arena opening ahead of the 2029 season.

Council member Diana Prince, who chaired the finance committee, said the deal protects taxpayers by capping the public contribution. "There is a hard ceiling on what the city will pay," she said after the vote. "If costs run over, the private partners absorb them, not the residents."

The two dissenting votes came from members who argued the money would be better spent on transit and housing. Council member Bruce Wayne called the arena "a stadium we cannot afford while buses run late and rents keep climbing."

Supporters countered that the arena will anchor a wider redevelopment of the riverfront district. City economists project the project will support roughly 2,400 construction jobs and several hundred permanent positions once the arena opens.

The debate stretched over three public hearings across the summer, drawing standing-room crowds. At the final hearing, more than sixty residents signed up to speak, with testimony running late into the evening. Supporters wore blue shirts reading "Build It"; opponents held signs demanding "Buses Before Basketball."

Under the approved financing, the public share will be funded through a mix of municipal bonds and a small surcharge on downtown parking, rather than a general tax increase. City finance staff estimate the bonds will be repaid over twenty years, with arena revenues and the parking surcharge covering the annual payments.

Several amendments were attached before the final vote. One requires the developer to publish quarterly cost reports. Another sets aside four million dollars a year for affordable-housing projects in adjacent neighborhoods, a concession that won over at least one previously undecided council member.

The mayor is expected to sign the budget into effect later this week. A separate vote on the surrounding transit improvements has been scheduled for next month, and council leaders have signaled that the two projects will be managed together to limit disruption to the riverfront during construction.
