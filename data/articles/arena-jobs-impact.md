---
title: Arena project promises jobs, but local businesses stay cautious
date: 2026-08-04
author: Clark Kent
section: Business
publication: Daily Planet
---
A day after the city council approved the downtown arena budget, business owners along the riverfront were weighing what the project means for them, with reactions ranging from optimism to unease.

The city projects around 2,400 construction jobs over the two-year build, drawing on local trade unions. "This is the kind of work that keeps skilled people in the city instead of chasing jobs elsewhere," said Maria Gonzales, who runs an electrical contracting firm three blocks from the site.

Not everyone is convinced the benefits will reach smaller shops. Owners of several cafes and hardware stores said they fear years of construction noise, blocked streets, and reduced foot traffic before any payoff arrives.

"Two years of dust and detours can close a small business long before the arena ever opens," said Sam Patel, who has run a corner grocery on Wharf Street for eleven years. He is asking the city for a support fund to help businesses survive the construction period.

Economists caution that arena projects often promise more than they deliver. A frequently cited concern is that spending inside an arena tends to replace, rather than add to, spending elsewhere in the city. The permanent job count, once construction ends, is expected to be in the low hundreds.

A study of comparable projects in five other cities found that arenas rarely pay for themselves through direct tax revenue alone. Their value, the study argued, lies in the surrounding development they can attract, which is far harder to predict and can take a decade or more to materialize.

City officials point to the riverfront's specific advantages: existing rail access, vacant land already zoned for mixed use, and a waterfront that has drawn developer interest for years. "This is not an arena dropped into a parking lot," said the city's economic development director. "It is the anchor of a district we have been trying to revive for a generation."

Local business groups say they will judge the project by whether the promised community benefits agreement is enforceable. A draft circulating this week includes hiring targets for local residents, a small-business relief fund, and penalties if the developer misses agreed milestones. Negotiations are expected to continue through the autumn.
