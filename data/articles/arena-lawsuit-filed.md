---
title: Community group sues to block arena bonds, alleges closed-door meeting
date: 2026-08-12
author: Lois Lane
section: City
publication: Daily Planet
---
A civic watchdog group filed suit in county court on Wednesday seeking to block the city from issuing bonds for the downtown arena, arguing council members violated the state's open-meetings law by holding a private briefing before last week's vote.

The complaint, filed by the Riverside Neighbors Coalition and docketed as Case No. 24-CV-1099, names the city council as defendant and asks a judge for an emergency injunction halting the bond sale until the claim is resolved.

According to the filing, at least five council members attended a closed-door briefing with arena developers two days before the public vote, without proper notice to residents. State law generally requires a quorum of any public body to deliberate in session open to the public, with narrow exceptions the coalition says do not apply here.

"Residents showed up to three hearings and spoke for hours believing they were being heard before a decision was made," said Priya Nair, the coalition's lead organizer. "If the real deal was struck behind closed doors first, that whole process was theater."

The city attorney's office said it had not yet been served with the complaint and declined to comment on the specific allegations, but noted that pre-vote briefings on project logistics are routine and, in the city's view, do not constitute deliberation requiring public notice.

Council member Diana Prince, who chaired the finance committee and attended the briefing in question, said the session covered construction scheduling and did not touch on the budget figures ultimately voted on. "Nothing about the number changed in that room," she said. "The vote was decided in public, on the record, like it should be."

Legal observers say open-meetings challenges to large public projects are common and rarely succeed in unwinding a completed vote, though they can delay financing while litigation proceeds. A hearing on the injunction request is expected within two weeks.

If the injunction is granted, the city could be barred from issuing the municipal bonds that fund its share of the arena budget until the underlying lawsuit is resolved, potentially pushing back the spring 2027 construction start.

The coalition said it is not opposed to the arena itself and would drop the suit if the city agreed to hold a supplemental public session reviewing the disputed briefing's contents. The mayor's office had not responded to that offer as of Wednesday evening.
