The following is the full statement issued by Mayor Perry White on Tuesday, following the city council's approval of the downtown arena budget.

"Last night the council took a decisive step toward the future of our riverfront. The new arena is more than a building. It is a signal that this city is ready to invest in itself, to bring events, jobs, and energy back to a district that has waited far too long for renewal.

I want to be clear about the safeguards. The public contribution is capped. Independent auditors will review the project's finances every quarter, and those reports will be published for every resident to read. Transparency is not optional on a project of this scale.

I also hear the concerns of those who voted no, and of the families who worry about transit and housing. These are not competing priorities. A stronger tax base from a thriving riverfront is exactly what funds better buses and more affordable homes. Next month's vote on transit improvements is the next piece of that same plan.

To the small business owners near the site: you will not be left to absorb the disruption alone. A support fund is being finalized, and hiring for the project will prioritize local residents.

Let me also address cost directly, because residents deserve straight answers. The public share will be repaid over twenty years through arena revenues and a modest downtown parking surcharge. It will not come from a general tax increase, and it will not come at the expense of essential services. If the independent audits ever show the project drifting from that commitment, I will say so publicly and we will correct course.

This has been a long and sometimes difficult debate, and that is exactly as it should be. Big decisions deserve scrutiny. I am grateful to the council members on both sides of the vote, and to the hundreds of residents who came to the hearings and made their voices heard.

I will sign this budget into effect this week. Let us build something this city can be proud of, responsibly and together."
